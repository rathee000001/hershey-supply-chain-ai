3 Co-encapsulation in double emulsion
Water-oil-water (W,/O/W2) emulsions consist of the internal aqueous droplets inside the oil
droplets, which are dispersed within the continuous aqueous phase. Emulsifiers are needed for
stabilizing two interfaces. Polyglycerol polyricinoleate is often used as a hydrophobic emulsifier
to stabilize W,/O emulsions, while proteins and protein/polysaccharide complexes can stabilize
the O/W interface (Evageliou, Panagopoulou, & Mandala, 2019; Li et al., 2012). The double
emulsions are attractive since they have potential uses for the encapsulation and fat reduction.
Polyphenols and anthocyanins from blueberry pomace were co-encapsulated in the W, phase
of double emulsions stabilized by WPI at the O/W2 interface (Bamba, et al., 2018). However,
commercial applications of W;/O/W2 emulsions are limited due to the migration between the
internal and external aqueous phases (Kumar, et al., 2022). Their physical stability has been
improved by controlling the osmotic stress balance between the internal and external aqueous
phases using hydrophilic solutes, increasing the viscosity or gelation of aqueous phases using
proteins and polysaccharides (Ji, et al., 2021; Oppermann, Renssen, Schuch, Stieger, & Scholten, Journal
2015; Yu et al., 2022). The internal aqueous phase and the oil phase of W,/O/W2 emulsions
provide advantageous microenvironment for encapsulating hydrophilic and hydrophobic BCs

Polysaccharide-based oleogels
The use of polysaccharides as a structuring agents, stabilizers, or thickening agents in water-
based systems is well established in the food industry. Moreover their GRAS status and
perception by consumers as food additive make them good candidates to structure edible oils
as well. Two main strategies for oil structuring using polysaccharides can be found in the
literature; direct or indirect [21~]. Considering the hydrophilic nature of most polysaccharides
derived from the hydroxyl content, the direct gelation of oil using these polymers is not
straightforward. Up-to-date only one polysaccharide, ethyl-cellulose, was discovered with the
ability to directly gel liquid oil. Therefore, the gelation of oil using polysaccharides is mainly
achieved using indirect methods such as emulsion template, high internal phase emulsion,
solvent exchange, drying, and freeze-drying.

3.2 Polyglycerol polyricinoleate replacement

(W/O), thus, PGPR has become the most widely use surfactant (Table 2). PGPR provides
sufficient stability to the primary emulsion (W/O) and has been accepted as food-grade

options. Therefore, few attempts have been done to eliminate partially or completely PGPR
from formulations and replace it with other surfactants that can stabilize the water-oil

5.6 Phenolic Compounds
Polyphenols constitute one of the most numerous and ubiquitous group of chemical
compounds (more than 8000 are currently known), representing the main antioxidants in the
human diet. Fruits, vegetables, cereals, pulses, wine, tea, coffee, and cocoa products are the
primary sources of phenolic compounds. They are mostly derivatives and/or isomers of
flavones, isoflavones, flavonols, catechins, anthocyanidins, and phenolic acids and possess a
high spectrum of biological properties including antiapoptosis, antiaging, anticarcinogen,
antiinflammation, antiatherosclerosis, cardiovascular protection, improvement of the
endothelial function as well as inhibition of angiogenesis and cell proliferation activity
(Bravo, 1998). These molecules are extremely unstable outside their natural environment.
Another unfortunate trait of phenolic compounds is their unpleasant taste (astringency). The
use of encapsulated polyphenols instead of free compounds can overcome these drawbacks as
well as improve the bioavailability. Fang and Bhandari (2010) have published an interesting
review about current research, developments, and trends of encapsulation of polyphenols.
Examples of encapsulated polyphenols and their incorporation to dairy foods are discussed.
Catechin is natural hydrophilic antioxidant molecule. Low bioavailability is a result of its limited
membrane permeability and degradation in digestion conditions. A study carried out by Aditya Book
etal. (2015) revealed an increase in the stability and bioaccessibility of catechin encapsulated ee
