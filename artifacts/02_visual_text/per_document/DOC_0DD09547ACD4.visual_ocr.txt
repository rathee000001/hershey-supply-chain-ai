Natural flavors are derived from natural sources— plants, animals, spices, fruits,

vegetables, roots, or fermentation products —and are isolated through physical,

enzymatic, or microbiological processes, according to FDA regulations. These

processes include extraction, distillation, and fermentation, which concentrate flavor

compounds. ® Rip Van +2

Here are the key sources and methods based on industry documentation:

1. Extraction Methods

Extraction uses solvents to dissolve and pull out flavor compounds from natural

sources. ® Rip Van

e Solvent Extraction: Involves soaking natural materials in water, alcohol, or other
solvents to create concentrated extracts, such as vanilla extract from vanilla beans.

e Cold Pressing: Primarily used for citrus fruits, this mechanical process crushes
peels to release essential oils.

e Enzymatic Extraction: Enzymes (e.g., #-glucosidase) are used to develop intense
aromas from sources like cherry or almond kernels.

e Supercritical Fluid Extraction: A modern, eco-friendly method, often using CO},
to extract flavors without leaving residual solvents.

e Sonication: Uses sound pressure to disrupt cell walls and increase the release of
flavor components. © Rip Van +5

2. Distillation Methods

Distillation separates compounds based on boiling points to isolate volatile aromatic

compounds. ® Rip Van

e Steam/Hydro Distillation: Plant material is heated with steam, causing essential
oils to release and then condense into a concentrated form (e.g., peppermint oil).

e Fractional Distillation: Used to further isolate specific components from a raw
extract, such as isolating vanillin from vanilla extract. © Rip Van +2

3. Fermentation & Biological Processes

Microorganisms are used to transform raw ingredients into complex flavor

compounds. ® Rip Van

e Microbial Fermentation: Bacteria, yeast, or fungi are used to convert
carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins into, for example, dairy-like, fruity, or nutty
flavors.

e Roasting/Heating: Natural materials are heated to create complex, savory, or
sweet flavor profiles (e.g., cocoa or coffee extracts). ® PubMed Central (PMC) (.gov) +4
