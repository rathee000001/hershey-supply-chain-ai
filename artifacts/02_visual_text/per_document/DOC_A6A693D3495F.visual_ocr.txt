Polyglycerol polyricinoleate (PGPR) and lecithin are commonly paired in chocolate
production, where lecithin reduces yield stress (viscosity) and PGPR acts as a powerful
flow enhancer, reducing the needed cocoa butter, cost, and bubbles. They act
synergistically to optimize flow, particularly in coating and molding

Key Details on PGPR + Lecithin Use:

e Synergy: While lecithin is excellent at reducing viscosity at lower shear rates, PGPR
is more effective at reducing yield stress (the pressure needed to make chocolate
flow), making them ideal to use together for optimized rheological properties.

¢ Production Benefits: Combining these emulsifiers, typically Lecithin (E322) and
PGPR (E476), enables chocolate to flow better into molds and reduces air bubbles.

e Cost Reduction: The combination allows manufacturers to reduce the amount of
expensive cocoa butter needed while maintaining fluidity.

¢ Typical Usage: Often found together in commercial chocolate, particularly in molded
bars and coatings, to ensure a smooth finish and efficient processing. (~) Patecista +3
